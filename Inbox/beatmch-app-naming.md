# beatmch app naming exploration

**Status:** ✅ **decided 2026-06-10 — the app is named Doppio.**
*Doppio movimento* — the score marking for "twice as fast" — plus the espresso
double-shot wink. Chosen over Twixt (runner-up) after the App Store search-lane
check below.

## The reframe (important)

`beatmch` reads as "beat match," but the app **isn't** a beatmatcher. Beatmatching = lining up two tracks' tempos. This app is a **half/double (×2 / ×½) tempo tool** — a living metronome that shows the same tempo at two related speeds. So the best name should point at *that relationship* (or the *flip* between speeds), not at "matching."

The goal landed on after a couple rounds: not a descriptive/generic name (Octa, Pulse, Halve felt too plain), but a **playful** name whose play actually *means something* — a payoff when you get it. Plain "Tempt" had charm but no reason behind it.

## Current shortlist

### Tempt — *the pull*
Reframed with meaning: the **temptation to flip**. Mid-set the app is the voice going "drop to half-time… no, double it for the drop." It lives in that pull between ×½ and ×2. So it's not just tempo-with-a-T — it names the *act* the app is built around.
- Pro: already liked for its playfulness; one-sentence story; fits the soul.
- Con: the meaning has to be *explained* — not self-evident from the word.

### Twixt — *the in-between*
Short for "betwixt" = *in between*. The app is the space **between** half and double — you live betwixt two tempos. Same old-word charm as Tempt, but the joke pays off the instant someone learns what it means. Faint candy-bar warmth (Twix).
- Pro: cleverness is self-evident; distinctive; meaning is baked into the word.
- Con: slightly archaic.

### Dubble — *the DJ wink*
"Dub" = real booth culture (dub tracks, dubplates) **and** literally derives from "double." A double-time tool called Dubble is a pun a DJ feels in the gut — and the doubled "b" is itself a tiny visual doubling.
- Pro: most distinctive / least generic; DJ-coded.
- Con: only names the "double" pole, not "half."

## Also considered (parked)
- **Octa / 8va** — doubling/halving a tempo is literally a musical octave (2:1 ratio); ×2/×½ = octave up/down. Accurate and quietly clever, but felt too general. `8va` (score marking for "up an octave", `8vb` for down) maps perfectly but is obscure / hard to search.
- **Halve** — names the default action (big number shows half), clean verb, but only names one direction.
- **Throb / Pulse** — names the *feel* (breathing rings, heartbeat). "Pulse" is very crowded in the App Store; "Throb" is freer but gives no hint of the half/double idea.
- **Beatmt** — the original playful compression; cute but compresses the *wrong* idea ("match").

## Leaning / recommendation
- **Tempt** if keeping the name already liked (now that it has a reason).
- **Twixt** if the cleverness should be self-evident without explaining it.

## 2026-06-10 — App Store reality check + Italian round

Checked the actual App Store search lane for each shortlist name (a name is only good
if people can find the app by typing it):

- **Tempt** — effectively taken: *Tempt: Romance Audiobooks* is established and owns
  the word in search. Discoverability would lean entirely on the subtitle.
- **Twixt** — nearly clear; only neighbour is *Twixt Time*, a dormant $9.99
  watch-accuracy app from 2013. A free real-English word — rare.
- **Dubble** — homophone swamp (Doubble dating, Double, Dubble Bubble Shooter,
  dubbii). Autocorrect fights the spelling forever. Effectively out.

Useful fact: the App Store subtitle (30 chars) is indexed for search, so a
non-descriptive name costs nothing if the subtitle carries the keywords
(e.g. "Half & double time BPM").

### New candidates — real musical Italian for half/double

- **Doppio** — *doppio movimento* is the actual score marking for "twice as fast";
  plus the espresso double-shot wink. Strongest payoff so far: not wordplay *about*
  the app, it's the literal instruction the app performs. Lane: a coffee-shop app
  cluster, none dominant — but espresso comes to mind before tempo.
- **Ottava** — Italian for *octave*; ×2/×½ IS the octave ratio (the parked 8va idea
  as a pronounceable word). Clearest lane of everything checked (one restaurant POS).
  Con: autocorrect/search confusion with "Ottawa."
- **Mezzo** — Italian for *half* and *middle* — carries both the default view (half)
  and the Twixt in-between idea. Lane: scattered small utilities. Con: names one pole.

Parked this round: **Duple** (real duple-meter term, but dry), **33/45** (the vinyl
speed switch — a perfect physical metaphor for one record at two speeds, but an
unsearchable name).

### Standing after this round
**Twixt** and **Doppio** lead: Twixt for the in-between idea + clearest-search,
Doppio for the deepest meaning. Tempt is out on search grounds; Dubble out on
spelling grounds.
