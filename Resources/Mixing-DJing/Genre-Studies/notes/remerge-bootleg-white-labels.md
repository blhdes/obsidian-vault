# Remerge & the White-Label Bootleg Mashup

A concept to dig into: the underground world of **bootleg white-label 12" mashups**, using the **Remerge** series as the entry point. Not a genre exactly — more a *format / culture* that cuts across genres.

## The vocabulary (plain words)

- **Bootleg** — an *unofficial* release: someone takes existing tracks, blends/re-edits them, and presses it themselves **without the original artists' or label's permission**.
- **White label** — a vinyl record with a **blank white paper label** in the centre (no printed artwork or text). Two reasons they exist: early promo/test pressings, and **bootleggers staying anonymous** (no names printed = harder to get sued). Consequence: there is usually **no cover art at all** — by design.
- **Catalog number** — a label's product code stamped on each release (e.g. `RE-001`). Lets collectors and databases (Discogs) identify a specific pressing.

## Remerge (the label)

- UK outfit that put out **limited bootleg 12" mashups ~2007–2009**.
- Normal run: **RE-001**, **RE-002**, **RE-003** (the "Volume 1/2/3" series).
- One odd special: **RE-XXX** — catalog `RE-XXX`, titled *"XXX"*, 2008, 12" white-label, listed on Discogs as an **Unofficial Release** with **zero images** (true to the white-label idea).

## Anchor track — "Arch Trak"

- **Burial vs Basic Channel — Arch Trak** (track 6 on RE-XXX).
- It's a mashup that fuses two worlds I'm already studying:
  - **Burial** → the hauntology / 2-step-garage-into-ambient lineage (see [[Resources/Mixing-DJing/Genre-Studies/ambient]]).
  - **Basic Channel** → the foundational **dub-techno** act: one detuned chord drowned in echo (see [[Resources/Mixing-DJing/Genre-Studies/dub-techno]]).
- So the bootleg literally welds *ambient/hauntology* onto a *dub-techno* engine — a neat case study in why mashup culture is interesting, not just novelty.

## Library handling

> When moving a Remerge / bootleg / white-label folder into `~/Music/Library/`, **keep the original folder name** (e.g. `XXX (2008) [MP3] {Remerge Records-RE-XXX}`). Like vinyl rips, the catalog/label info in the folder name is the only reliable way to re-identify these — they have no clean cover or canonical tags to fall back on.
