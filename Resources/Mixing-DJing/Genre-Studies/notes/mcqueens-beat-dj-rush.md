# McQueen's Beat — DJ Rush

## Essence
Relentless, looping Chicago drum-machine workout. The kind of track that proves you don't need chord changes to hypnotize a room — just a great pattern that refuses to let go.

## Where it's from
- **Release:** *Traks Couture* (1998) — 4-track 12" EP (catalog **DJAX-UP-304**)
- **Label:** **Djax-Up-Beats** — Eindhoven, Netherlands, run by Saskia Slegers (**Miss Djax**)
- **Artist:** DJ Rush (Issa Rasheed), Chicago-born, later Berlin-based
- **Era:** late-90s — DJ Rush channelling the Chicago **ghetto house** sound (Dance Mania lineage) onto a Dutch label, a couple of years before his Berlin hard-techno turn

## Why it grabs
- Pure **groove-as-architecture** — no melody, no harmonic arc, just drum machines and chant samples locked into a tight loop.
- The track *lives* on tension you don't realize is there until a layer drops out or a filter opens. Classic Chicago trick.
- Title most likely nods to **Alexander McQueen** (the fashion designer), not Steve McQueen. The release is called *Traks Couture* — "couture" is fashion-world language, and by **1998** Alexander McQueen was *the* name in fashion: British Designer of the Year in both 1996 and 1997, and creative director of **Givenchy** since October 1996. To confirm the reading: check whether other track titles on the EP also reference designers (Versace, Gaultier, Chanel, etc.).

## Style & technical notes
- **BPM:** roughly 135–140 (typical for DJ Rush's 90s ghetto/hard house).
- **Drum sources:** Roland **TR-909** kick/hat + **TR-808** clap/cowbell territory. Raw, dry, very little reverb.
- **Structure:** loop-based, additive. Layers come in and out rather than verses/choruses.
- **Mix aesthetic:** intentionally lo-fi — raw, clipped, distorted and proud of it; typical of this ghetto/hard-house era.
- **DJ utility:** great long mixer tool. Easy to loop, easy to layer under or over another track. The hat pattern is distinctive enough to be recognizable even at low volume in a transition.

## Context — why DJ Rush matters here
- This is **early DJ Rush** — before he moved to Berlin and pivoted to the **hard techno / schranz** sound he's now famous for.
- *Traks Couture* sits in the same conversation as **Dance Mania** records by DJ Funk, DJ Deeon, Paul Johnson, Parris Mitchell. If you love McQueen's Beat, that whole catalog is your next stop.
- The line from this 1998 sound to his later 150-BPM Berlin output is direct: same skeletal philosophy (drums + chant + relentlessness), just sped up and harder.
