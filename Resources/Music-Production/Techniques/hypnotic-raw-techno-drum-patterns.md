# Hypnotic & Raw Techno Drum Pattern Types

A survey of drum-pattern *archetypes* for raw/hypnotic techno — a menu to pick from, not a build session. Some are already in the toolkit from earlier sessions; others are genuinely new territory.

## Already in the toolkit

No re-teaching here — just naming them as pattern *types* so they can be picked deliberately instead of reached for by accident.

| Type | What it does | Where used |
|---|---|---|
| **Rolling interlock** | Kick + sub + perc each claim their own slot, zero overlap — forward motion from complementary timing, not density | [[kick-sub-interlock-sidechain\|Kick/Sub Interlock]] — Hardgroove 134 |
| **Polymeter drift** | A loop whose length doesn't divide the bar (3 beats over 4) — realigns every few bars, hypnosis from slow rotation | [[polymeter-percussion\|Polymeter Percussion]] |
| **Stripped/subtractive** | Same base pattern, one hit removed on alternating bars — sparser without being a different pattern | [[loop-character-variations\|Loop Character Variations]] — Hardgroove 134 Loop 3 |
| **Ghost-note micro-groove** | Quiet (vel 20–40) hits on `e`/`a` — felt, not consciously heard | [[ghost-notes-and-fills\|Ghost Notes & Fills]] |

## New territory

### 1. Odd-length polymeter, sixteenth resolution

The polymeter note flagged this and parked it: instead of a loop measured in **beats** (3-beat clip, realigns every 3 bars), measure it in **sixteenths** (5 or 7-sixteenth clip). It drifts faster and less predictably — realignment takes 5 bars (for a 5-sixteenth loop) instead of 3.

```
         1 e & a 2 e & a 3 e & a 4 e & a|1 e & a 2 e & a 3 e & a 4 e & a
Kick   : ● · · · ● · · · ● · · · ● · · ·|● · · · ● · · · ● · · · ● · · ·
Perc-5 : ● · · · · ● · · · · ● · · · · ●|· · · · ● · · · · ● · · · · ● ·
```

*(perc = one hit every 5 sixteenths — bar 1 hits land on `1`/`e`(2)/`&`(3)/`a`(4); bar 2 has already shifted to `2`/`e`(3)/`&`(4). Same "one polymeter element per sketch" rule applies — two rotating loops fight each other.)*

### 2. Rolling / non-kick-locked groove

Hardgroove 134's whole approach so far has been **call-and-response placement** — every element takes its own turn, nothing overlaps. This is the opposite device: a continuous soft 16th roll (a shaker, a filtered noise tick) that runs underneath *everything*, with a handful of louder accents placed deliberately off the kick's quarters. The kick still anchors the floor; the roll doesn't reinforce it, it argues with it.

```
         1 e & a 2 e & a 3 e & a 4 e & a
Kick   : ● · · · ● · · · ● · · · ● · · ·
Roll   : ◦ ◦ ● ◦ ◦ ◦ ● ◦ ◦ ● ◦ ◦ ◦ ◦ ● ◦
```

*(◦ = continuous soft roll, ● = accent — none of the accents sit on the kick's `1 2 3 4`. This reads as more tribal/rolling than the interlock style; genuinely different feel, not a replacement for it.)*

### 3. Call & response, percussion-only

The call-and-response idea already used for the LEAD motif ([[bleep-lead-motif|Bleep Lead & Motif Writing]]), applied to two percussion voices instead of a melody: element A plays a short phrase in bars 1–2, element B answers with a different phrase in bars 3–4 (or every half-bar, for a tighter trade). No new mechanic — same idea, different material.

### 4. Rhythmic found-sound hits

ATMOS (Hardgroove 134, track 6) used a captured found-audio fragment *texturally* — sparse, scattered, off-grid. This type is the same source material used the opposite way: a metallic clang, a machine hit, a filtered noise burst, placed **on the grid**, doing a percussion part's actual job (accenting a beat or a 16th) instead of floating over the top. Distinct from ATMOS because it's rhythm, not atmosphere — though both can come from the same recording session.

## Why these lean hypnotic/raw

Hypnotic techno gets its pull from **long, near-unchanging exposure** rather than pattern complexity — polymeter drift and the rolling groove both generate movement *without* adding new parts or changing the loop length, which is what keeps a pattern danceable for 8+ minutes without feeling static or busy. This is a genre note, not a rule — plenty of techno (and non-techno) uses denser, more event-driven patterns instead.

## 🎹 Homework

Pick one to actually hear:
- **Odd-length polymeter** — duplicate HATS or PERC onto a spare track, set its clip Length to `0.1.1` (one bar + one sixteenth = 5 sixteenths) instead of a whole-bar length, loop it against the existing kick, listen across 5 bars.
- **Rolling groove** — on a new track, draw a continuous 16th pattern at low velocity (20–40) under the existing beat, then raise 3–4 hits per bar to full velocity, avoiding the kick's exact quarters.

Either is a 5–10 minute experiment — no new device, just placement and clip length.
