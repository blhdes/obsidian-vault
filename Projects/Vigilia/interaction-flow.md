# The interaction flow

The single session of [[Vigilia|Vigilia]], beat by beat, with the reasoning under each beat preserved. The reasoning matters more than the steps: the steps are easy to redraw, but the *why* is what keeps a redraw from quietly breaking the atmosphere.

## The shape of the session, and why it isn't one of the obvious two

Two obvious shapes were both rejected:

- **A rigid multi-phase wizard** ("step 3 of 4", next / back). Rejected for being **too clinical**. Visible machinery kills the atmosphere; the moment the user sees a progress indicator, they are using software, not keeping a vigil.
- **A fully blank canvas.** Rejected for the opposite failure: a blank page **leaves the user alone with their anger** and never pushes them toward the second movement, the well-wishing — which is where all the value of the app actually is. Freedom here just becomes a place to stew.

The resolution is a **"canvas guided by a single phrase."** The structure is real, but it lives in **language, not in screens**. There is one surface throughout; what changes is the words on it. The scaffolding is present and invisible at the same time.

## 1. The changing welcome line

The app opens with a **changing welcome line** — a different seed-phrase **each time** you open it, the way Claude desktop greets you differently on each launch.

This phrase is doing the real work of the app. It is the **scaffolding, made invisible**. Instead of announcing "step 1", it **plants a seed and trusts you** to follow it. This is the mechanism by which the structure can live in the language rather than the interface: the guidance is a sentence, not a screen. The bank of these phrases still needs to be written — see [[open-questions|open questions]] — in a quasi-liturgical cadence that never names a tradition.

## 2. The single writing canvas — naming the wound

Beneath the welcome line is a **single writing canvas**. Here the user **freely names the wound**: who hurt them, and how. No fields, no prompts beyond the seed. This first movement is honest naming, not analysis.

## 3. The swipe down — a voluntary closing gesture

To move on, the user performs a deliberate **swipe down**. The way this transition is triggered was a specific choice:

- **Not** an automatic trigger on a typing pause. That is **too fragile** — people stop typing to *think* mid-sentence all the time, and an app that advances on its own would betray them at exactly the wrong moment.
- **A voluntary act instead.** The voluntariness is the point, and it is *ritual*, not just UX safety: **the user decides when they are done naming the harm.** Closure should be chosen, not detected.

The **direction** is also not arbitrary. Swiping *down* **embodies the metaphor**: you physically push the wound downward, out of the light. The gesture *is* the meaning.

At the moment of locking, there is a **single low haptic beat** — one soft thud, felt in the hand. The closure should be **felt, not merely seen**. This is the body's confirmation that the first movement is sealed.

## 4. The lock and the dimming

The swipe **locks the wound text as read-only and dims it**: its luminosity drops and it **recedes into the penumbra**, the surrounding dark. This single move does two jobs at once:

- **Technically**, it separates the two movements of the session **without ever leaving the canvas** — no new screen, no modal, no navigation. One surface, two states.
- **Symbolically**, it says: *the wound is already spoken; do not ruminate on it; now look forward.* You **cannot re-edit your anger** — it is locked. The only direction left is downward and onward, toward the light below.

There is a quiet structural benefit here too: because the grievance becomes read-only, the app **cannot become a loop of rewriting the grievance**. It physically forecloses the rumination spiral. You said it once; that is enough.

## 5. The second seed — the loving-kindness beat

On a **new line in the same canvas** — a structured line break, **one continuous flow**, not a new screen — a **second seed appears as a faint placeholder**, something like *"Now, wish them something good…"*. It **vanishes the moment the user starts typing**, exactly like the welcome placeholder did.

The whole point of this beat is what it is *not*: it is **not a modal and not a new screen**. It is just a change of placeholder text in the same writing surface. Keeping it inside one canvas is deliberate — it reinforces that this is **one movement of the heart, not two separate tasks**. The journey from wound to well-wishing happens **without a seam**. If this were two screens, it would feel like two chores; as one flowing canvas, it feels like a single turning.

## 6. The well-wishing

The user writes the **well-wishing** on that new line, in the **luminous zone** — at full light, while the wound above sits dimmed. The contrast is doing emotional work for free (see [[aesthetic-direction|the aesthetic direction]]: the light emerges from the text, so the dimmed wound and the bright well-wishing are literally different brightnesses on the same black field).

## 7. The release — transmutation / ascension

Then the user **releases**, and the **transmutation / ascension** plays. The **words themselves come apart into light** and **ascend like embers or luminous motes**, dissolving into the upper darkness. A flame that rises and goes out above.

What it must **not** be: **cosmic confetti, colored particles, anything festive or "magical."** That would shatter the mood instantly. The animation is **restrained, and it is moving *because* it is restrained.** This is the visual realization of the core metaphor — the note rises and is offered, it is not wiped. See [[ephemerality-and-transmutation|Ephemerality & transmutation]] for why this framing is non-negotiable. The exact technique (a particle system vs. dissolving the actual glyphs) is still [[open-questions|open]].

## 8. The void

Finally, the screen returns to **total black**. The note **exists nowhere** — not stored, not sent anywhere, not recoverable. The session is complete in the most literal sense: there is nothing left of it. The user is returned to the dark they started in, having made the turn and let it go.

---

Every transition above is **slow, at the tempo of a breath** — see the motion note in [[aesthetic-direction|the aesthetic direction]]. Nothing here should ever feel snappy or responsive; the app holds a space, it does not react quickly.
