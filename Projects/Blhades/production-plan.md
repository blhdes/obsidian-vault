# Production Plan

How the White Canvas collection gets physically made without producing stock upfront. Status: **TPOP tested and rejected (2026-06-10) — print area too small/cropped**. Priorities reset: **quality and print freedom over price**. Expand as decisions land.

## The model

No bulk production. Each shirt is printed **only when a customer pays** (print on demand / POD). More expensive per unit than a bulk run, but zero inventory risk — the right trade-off while the goal is first customers, not margin. Paying more per shirt for a better printer is explicitly acceptable.

- Single-unit orders double as samples for photoshoots / personal wear.
- Keeps the Edition of 50 framing intact — the cap is a promise, not pre-made stock.

## Hard requirement: large print area

Several designs occupy **almost the entire front of the shirt**. Standard POD direct-to-garment (DTG) platens are ~30×40 cm — that's what made TPOP feel "basic and cropped." Any producer that can't print near-full-front is **discarded**, regardless of price.

Reference numbers found so far:
- Typical POD standard: ~30×40 cm → **not enough**.
- Inkthreadable (UK): 32×50 cm DTG → borderline, width may still crop.
- Live.ink (UK): DTG up to 40 cm wide; screen print 37×47 cm → closest so far.
- True full-front freedom usually means **screen printing or DTF (direct-to-film transfer)** rather than standard DTG.

**First question to every candidate, before anything else:** "What is your maximum print size on a heavyweight tee, front placement?" — send the real artwork dimensions.

## Priority: one partner for garment + print

Whenever possible, the producer and the printer should be **the same company**. Splitting them (buy blanks here, print there) means double shipping, double lead time, no single party accountable for the finished shirt, and a worse per-unit cost. Blanks-only suppliers (WSYD, Studio Innate, Rue Porter, UNBND) are therefore **benchmarks or fallbacks, not the plan** — full-package candidates (Create Fashion Brand, René Bassett, ASBX, Portugal Textile, t-shirt-oversize.fr, BLANKS WORKS) rank first.

## Parked for later: site copy vs garment

Not a focus now (we're collecting producers/printers first). For the record: the site spec line ("combed cotton · cut in Portugal") doesn't match the Freestyler blank (open-end carded, made in Bangladesh). Revisit once the producer is chosen — a Portuguese producer makes it moot.

## Fabric — what "couture-grade" actually means

Three layers decide how luxurious a tee feels. Weight (gsm) is the one everyone talks about, but it's the least important of the three.

### 1. Yarn (the biggest lever)
- **Open-end / carded** — cheapest, fastest spinning. Fuzzy surface, dry/rough hand, raises lint. The Freestyler is this, on purpose ("dry handfeel").
- **Ring-spun** — fibers aligned and twisted. Smoother, stronger, cleaner print surface.
- **Combed ring-spun** — short fibers combed out before spinning. Softer still, more refined. This is the floor for "luxury".
- **Compact-spun** — ring-spun with the fibers compressed even tighter. Cleanest face, least pilling. What top-tier tees use.

### 2. Weight (gsm = grams per square metre)
- 240 gsm = today's **premium streetwear standard** (the Fear of God Essentials zone — the tee "stands, doesn't hang").
- 300–400 gsm = where high-end/couture tees are pushing now.
- But gsm ≠ quality: a 240 gsm open-end tee feels rougher than a 220 gsm combed ring-spun one. Heavy + cheap yarn = cardboard.

### 3. Finishing (where the "expensive hand" comes from)
- **Garment dyeing** — dye the sewn shirt, not the fabric roll → depth of colour, vintage character, pre-shrunk.
- **Enzyme / bio wash** — enzymes eat the surface fuzz → clean, smooth face.
- **Silicone wash** — the buttery, slippery hand premium tees have.
- **Tumble finishing** — relaxes the fibres after cutting/sewing.
- Luxury production stacks these (e.g. garment-dyed + enzyme + silicone).

### The BLH*DES target spec (to quote to producers)
> 100% GOTS organic cotton, **combed ring-spun or compact-spun**, single jersey, **240–300 gsm**, boxy/relaxed cut, garment-dyed black / off-white, enzyme + silicone finish, custom neck label.

This is precisely ASBX territory: in-house fabric R&D (they developed a 600 gsm French terry), GOTS organics, vintage washes/garment dyeing, screen printing — in Barcelos, inside the Braga–Guimarães–Barcelos textile triangle where premium jersey mills (e.g. Valérius, 25k pieces/day for international brands) cluster. The "cut in Portugal couture supplier" story is geographically real there.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Front-only (1 screen) | ~€27 | €13 | €23 | €33 | €43 |

What this reframed:
- **€40 is the danger zone, not €50.** Front+back @€40 ≈ €8/unit — fees + one return wipe it out. But @€50 front+back still nets ~€18/unit (**~€350 surplus** on 20) — enough to fund a cheaper next drop. The earlier "€40–50 = zero/negative benefit" worry was too pessimistic; only €40 truly is.
- **Tier by production cost, don't flat-price.** Front+back genuinely costs ~€5 more to make than a 1-screen front-only → hero/front+back pieces €60–75, entry/front-only pieces ~€50. Same Edition of 50; pricing tiers are normal in fashion, not a compromise.
- **"Luxury never lowers price" was being applied backwards.** The rule forbids *discounting an already-launched price* (that signals desperation). Launching lower and *raising* later — early buyers got in first, the brand visibly appreciates — is the safe direction. Launching at the €70 ceiling with no proof and being *forced* to discount is the actual disaster. **Up is safe, down is fatal** → start REPENT at €60, climb on later pieces.

Chosen: **REPENT @ €60** — lead with the strongest seller (the best answer to "will it sell?" is the most sellable piece per the commercial ranking, not the cheapest), at a premium-but-approachable price. ~€28/unit margin → **~€450 surplus** on 20.

### Legal/tax roadmap (the two IVAs — clarified 2026-06-11)

Two different IVAs, often confused:

- **IVA we PAY (on Wrong Culture's invoice):** unavoidable. As a private individual = final consumer, the +21% is simply part of the cost (~€69–88 per pressing). Only a registered business can deduct/recover it.
- **IVA we'd CHARGE (on sales):** only businesses add IVA. Selling as a particular, the €40 is the €40 — but recurring sales of new self-produced goods is **by definition economic activity** in Spain, however small.

The key distinction most people miss — these are **two separate registrations**:

1. **Alta censal (modelo 036/037, Hacienda)** — registers the activity with the tax office. **Free.** Allows legal invoicing, charging IVA on sales, and **deducting the IVA paid to Wrong Culture** (the €69–88/pressing comes back). Requires quarterly IVA returns.
2. **Autónomo (RETA, Seguridad Social)** — the expensive part (~€200/month; tarifa plana ~€87 first year). Court doctrine says it's not required while the activity isn't habitual and income stays below the minimum wage → many small side projects run "alta in Hacienda, no RETA." Tolerated gray zone, not a guarantee.

**The roadmap:** first pressing as a private individual (eat the purchase IVA, sell quietly — under the DAC7 thresholds noted below). If drops become recurring → do the **alta censal** (costs nothing, saves the purchase IVA) and book one session with a **gestor (~€50–100)** to set it up right. RETA only when income justifies it. Spain has no small-business IVA exemption regime in force yet (the EU franchise scheme was pending transposition) — re-check when registering.

## Print method notes

- **DTG** = inkjet on fabric. Fine detail, no minimums, but platen-size limits and large solid whites on black can look less dense.
- **Screen print** = the streetwear standard. Densest ink, biggest sizes, but per-design setup cost → shines at batch size (ASBX route).
- **DTF** = printed transfer film, heat-pressed. Large sizes, vivid on dark fabric, slightly different hand-feel (sits on the fabric).
- Test verdicts should come from the most demanding design: **VII — The World Is On Fire** (near-full-front).
