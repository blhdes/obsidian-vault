# Detroit Chord Stab

A **stab** = a full chord played as one short percussive hit. It works as a *rhythm* instrument that happens to carry harmony — not a melody, not a pad. The Detroit signature is a **minor 9th chord** ([[../Theory-Basics/sevenths-and-ninths|Am9 = A C E G B]]) stabbed on syncopated positions.

## The sound

- **Polyphonic synth — Voices ≥ 5.** The exact opposite of the mono bass rule: five notes must sound at once.
- **Fast attack, short-ish release** so the hit is percussive; a hint of release tail is fine.
- Too bright/cheesy? **Close the low-pass filter partway** — Detroit stabs are often dark and filtered, felt as much as heard.
- Preset hunt: search "stab" first; otherwise any synth keys/poly preset works with the envelope/filter tweaks above.

## The voicing

Mid-range, sitting **above the bass, below the hats**: A3 C4 E4 G4 B4.

Optional classic move: **drop the root (A3) from the chord** — the bassline already owns A, so the stab only adds the color notes. Try both; with root = fuller, without = airier.

## The rhythm

Sparse and on **weak positions** (same [[syncopated-bassline|syncopation]] logic as the bass — the stab answers it, never lands with the kick):

```
         1 e & a 2 e & a 3 e & a 4 e & a
Stab   : · · ● · · · · ● · · · · · · ● ·
```

- All hits play the *same* chord — the rhythm is the interest, not chord changes.
- Short (1/16) note lengths; slight velocity differences between the three hits.
- **Same swing groove file on the clip** (the `a`-of-2 hit needs to move with the hats).

## Workflow: drawing stacked notes

Draw the 5-note chord once at the first position, lasso-select the stack, then **Option-drag** copies of it to the other positions.
