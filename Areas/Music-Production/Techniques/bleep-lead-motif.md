# Bleep Lead & Motif Writing

A club-track melody is not a song's vocal line — it's a **motif**: a 3–5 note cell, surrounded by space, repeated with small variations. The repetition makes it hypnotic; the variation keeps it alive.

## Call and response

The oldest melody structure there is: bar 1 asks, bar 2 answers.

```
Bar 1    1 e & a 2 e & a 3 e & a 4 e & a
Lead   : · A · · · · C · · E · · · · · ·      ← call (rises away from home)

Bar 2    1 e & a 2 e & a 3 e & a 4 e & a
Lead   : · G · · · · E · · A · · · · · ·      ← response (falls back to A)
```

(A4 C5 E5 → G5 E5 A4 — all [[../Theory-Basics/a-minor-pentatonic|A minor pentatonic]], octave 5 region.)

Three craft rules hiding in it:

1. **Keep the rhythm, change the notes.** Both bars use the same `e / & / e` rhythm — the variation lives in pitch only. Change one dimension at a time.
2. **Space is part of the melody.** Beat 4 stays empty in both bars. Sparse = hypnotic; busy = exhausting (style dial: bleep-techno sparse, trance busy).
3. **End the response on the root.** Landing back on A closes the loop, so the 2-bar cycle feels like breathing.

## Register: everyone owns a lane

| Lane | Who |
|---|---|
| Sub/low | Kick, bass (A1) |
| Mid | Stab + pad (A3–B4) |
| **Top** | **Lead (octave 5)** |
| Noise/top | Hats |

The lead sits *above* the stab's highest note so nothing fights.

## Sound: a bleep from scratch

First patch built by hand (all known dials, on Drift): **sine or triangle oscillator, fast attack, short decay/release, mono**. That's the entire bleep recipe — the plainness of the waveform is the aesthetic. Rhythm placement on weak positions → **same swing groove file** on the clip.
