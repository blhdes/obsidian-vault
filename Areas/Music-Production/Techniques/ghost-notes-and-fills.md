# Ghost Notes & Fills

Two tools that make a drum loop feel *played* instead of programmed. They work at different zoom levels: **ghost notes** breathe every bar (micro), **fills** breathe every phrase (macro — the [[techno-production-arc|8-bar rule]] in drum form). Genre-neutral; the examples below are techno-flavored.

## Ghost notes

A ghost note is a **very quiet hit between the main hits** — felt as roll and pull, not consciously heard. The whole trick is **velocity**: the same note at full velocity is just a busier pattern; at ~20–40 it becomes texture.

```
         1 e & a 2 e & a 3 e & a 4 e & a
Kick   : ● · · · ● · · · ● · · · ● · · ·
Ride   : · · ● · · · ● · · · ● · · · ● ·
Ghost  : · · · ◦ · ◦ · · · · · ◦ · · · ·
```

Rules of thumb:

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Velocity | **20–40** (main hits 90–127) |
| Position | `e`/`a` 16ths only — never on the kick's quarters |
| Density | sparse: 2–4 per bar |
| Sound | a soft short perc (e.g. *Perc Hit Soft*) or the snare itself, quiet |

Bonus: ghosts on `e`/`a` are exactly the material a **Swing 16ths groove** needs to act on ([[swing-and-groove|known rule]]) — ghosts + swing switch on together.

## Fills

A fill is a **deliberate variation at the end of a phrase** (every 4 or 8 bars) that signals "the loop restarts here". Techno fills are subtle — a flourish, not a drum solo.

**Building one in Live:** the main drum clip must be longer than 1 bar. Use the **Duplicate button** in the Clip panel twice (1 → 2 → 4 bars — content copies automatically), then edit **only the last beat(s) of bar 4**.

Techno fill vocabulary (pick ONE per fill):

- **Snare/clap roll** — 16ths on the last 1–2 beats with a **rising velocity ramp**
- **Kick drop** — delete the beat-4 kick of bar 4 (a one-beat gasp before the restart)
- **Perc run** — a quick tom/tube-hit sequence walking down the last beat
- **Let-ring** — an open hat/crash on the last `&` sustaining into bar 1

## Gotchas

- Ghosts at full velocity = clutter. When in doubt, quieter.
- A fill every bar stops being a fill — every 4 or 8 bars keeps it an event.
- After editing bar 4, A/B against the old 1-bar clip: the groove should feel alive, not busier.
