# Open vs Closed Hi-Hat (and the Techno Offbeat Pattern)

The hi-hat is the **engine** of the beat. Its rhythm is what makes a groove *move*. The two flavors — **closed** and **open** — are the same physical cymbal pair, just played differently. Combining them in a specific way is what gives **techno (and house)** its characteristic forward-pulling feel.

## The two sounds

| Type | What it is | What it sounds like | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Closed hi-hat** | Cymbals pressed shut, struck | Short, sharp `tss` | Cuts off instantly |
| **Open hi-hat** | Cymbals loose, struck | Long, sizzling `tssss` | Rings until the next hat (or you mute it) |

In a drum kit (real or sampled), they share the same "voice" — when you hit a closed hat, it usually **cuts off** any ringing open hat. That's a physical fact (one set of cymbals) that drum machines replicate. Good to know: it means a closed-hat *right after* an open-hat will "choke" the open-hat tail.

## The techno offbeat pattern

The most recognizable techno groove pattern uses **closed hats on the beat** and **open hats on the offbeat**:

```
         1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &
Kick   : ● · ● · ● · ● ·
Clap   : · · ● · · · ● ·
ClsHat : ● · ● · ● · ● ·
OpnHat : · ● · ● · ● · ●
```

- `●` = hit, `·` = silence
- Each column = 1/8 note

What you hear: `boom-tss-TSSSS-boom-CLAP-TSSSS-boom-tss-TSSSS-boom-CLAP-TSSSS`

The trick is that the **open hat lands on the `&`** (the offbeat — the space *between* the kicks). It sizzles for half a beat, then the next closed hat (or kick) cuts it. That sizzle bouncing back and forth with the kick is what creates the forward-leaning, "I want to dance" pulse.

## Why it works

- The **kick pins down the pulse** (where the beat *is*).
- The **open hat sits in the gap** between kicks — it fills the silence and pulls your ear *forward* toward the next kick.
- The constant alternation creates a **call-and-response** at the smallest rhythmic level.

Take the open hat away and you're left with a stiff, robotic loop. Add it on the offbeat and the whole thing breathes.

## In Ableton (Drum Rack note numbers)

In a standard Drum Rack, drum pieces are mapped to MIDI notes (you don't need to memorize these — just hover the pad):

| Piece | Default note |
|---|---|
| Kick | **C1** |
| Snare | **D1** |
| Clap | **D#1** |
| Closed Hi-Hat | **F#1** |
| Open Hi-Hat | **A#1** |

So in the MIDI editor, the open-hat row sits a few rows above the closed-hat row.

## Style notes (when it's *not* offbeat opens)

- **House** uses the same offbeat-open-hat trick — basically borrowed it from disco.
- **Hip-hop / trap** uses opens differently — sparingly, more decorative.
- **Drum & bass / breakbeat** uses chopped breaks — different language entirely.

For now: offbeat opens = the techno / house engine.
