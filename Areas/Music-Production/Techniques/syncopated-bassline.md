# Syncopated Bassline

**Syncopation** = putting emphasis on the *weak* positions of the bar (the offbeats and the `e`/`a` 16ths) instead of the strong beats. The ear expects weight on 1-2-3-4; when accents land between them instead, the line feels like it's *pulling forward*. Genre-neutral concept — Detroit/house bass just uses a lot of it.

## From static to moving

The sketch-01 bass was 4 identical offbeat notes — correct interlock ([[kick-bass-interlock|kick/bass interlock]]), zero movement. A syncopated line keeps the interlock but varies **where** hits land and **which** notes play:

```
         1 e & a 2 e & a 3 e & a 4 e & a
Bass   : · · A · · · A · · · A A · · C G
```

- **Anchor:** offbeat `&` hits on the root (A1) — the known interlock.
- **The push:** the extra hit on the `a` of 3 — a 16th *early anticipation* of beat 4. This is the syncopation.
- **The turnaround:** C2 → G1 at the bar's end, resolving back to A on the next bar's first hit. Movement without leaving [[../Theory-Basics/a-minor-pentatonic|A minor pentatonic]].

## Design rules

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Root note (A) on most hits | Detroit bass *moves* but stays rooted; neighbors are seasoning |
| Extra notes from the pentatonic (A C D E G) | Guaranteed to fit the key |
| New hits go on weak positions (`e`/`a`), never on the kick's quarters | Keeps the interlock AND creates the syncopated pull |
| Short (1/16) staccato lengths | Punchy techno feel; long notes smear into the kick |
| Mono voice, sits ~A1 | Known from sketch 01 |

## Groove must match

**Drag the same swing groove onto the bass clip.** The bass now has `e`/`a` hits; if the hats swing and the bass doesn't, the two flam against each other at every push. Same groove file = drums and bass breathe together. (This is the general rule: one groove for every rhythmic clip in the project.)
