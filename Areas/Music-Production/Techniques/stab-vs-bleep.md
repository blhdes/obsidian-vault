# Stab vs Bleep

Quick reference — the two mid/top "musical identity" elements compared. Full recipes: [[detroit-chord-stab|Detroit Chord Stab]] and [[bleep-lead-motif|Bleep Lead & Motif Writing]].

| | **Stab** | **Bleep** |
|---|---|---|
| Notes | One **chord** (3–5 notes at once) | **Single notes**, one at a time (mono) |
| Job | Rhythm — one chord punched on weak positions | Melody — a small motif (call & response) |
| Register | Mid (A3–B4) | Top (octave 5) |
| Synth | Poly, ≥5 voices, dark/filtered | Mono, sine/triangle, fast attack |

Same DNA — short envelope, sits above the bass. The stab is **harmonic percussion**; the bleep is the **melodic hook**.

**Do you need either?** No. Harder/rawer club techno often runs on drums + rumble + bass alone, with identity coming from percussion, texture, and variation instead of melody/harmony. A stab or bleep is one *option* for a track's identity element, not a requirement. (See [[techno-production-arc|the arc]], stage 2: 1–3 musical parts *max* — zero melodic ones is a valid choice.)
