# Kick / Bass Interlock (Offbeat Bass Pattern)

In dance music, **the kick and the bass share the same low-frequency space**. If they hit at the same time, they fight — the mix gets muddy and loses punch. The trick is to make them **take turns**: kick on the beat, bass in the gaps between kicks. This is the *single most important rhythmic relationship* in techno and house.

## The pattern

The classic foundation — kick on every 1/4, bass on every offbeat 1/8 (the "&" between beats):

```
              1 e & a 2 e & a 3 e & a 4 e & a
Kick        : ● · · · ● · · · ● · · · ● · · ·
Bass        : · · ● · · · ● · · · ● · · · ● ·
```

- `●` = hit, `·` = silence
- 16 columns = 16 sixteenth notes in a bar (the "e", "&", "a" subdivisions)
- Bass plays exactly **4 notes per bar**, perfectly in the middle of each kick gap

What you hear: `BOOM-bass-BOOM-bass-BOOM-bass-BOOM-bass`. Push and pull. Conversation.

## Why it works

Two reasons:

**1. Frequency / amplitude.** A kick drum is a brief, loud burst of low-frequency energy (60–100 Hz). A bass note is *sustained* low-frequency energy. If both hit at once, two big low-end sources collide → distortion, mud, loss of definition. Splitting them in time keeps each one clean and audible.

**2. Groove / momentum.** The kick is *down* (downbeat). The bass on the "&" is *up* (upbeat). The constant down-up-down-up creates the dance pulse — it's the same physical alternation as walking.

## Note length matters

How *long* each bass note lasts changes the feel a lot:

| Bass note length | Feel | Style |
|---|---|---|
| Short (1/16, staccato) | Punchy, tight, leaves space | Techno, minimal |
| Medium (1/8) | Smooth, fills more space | House, deep house |
| Long (held into next kick) | Wash, almost pad-like | Trance, prog |

For techno: **start with short 1/16-length notes**. Each bass note hits, decays quickly, then the next kick lands. Tight and dry.

In Ableton's MIDI editor, **drag the right edge of a note** to change its length. The default Pencil tool draws 1/16-length notes — perfect.

## Pitch: which note?

For a one-note bassline (totally normal in techno), use the **root note of the key**.

For [[techno-sketch-01|our sketch in A minor]], that's **A**. We sit it at **A1** — one octave below middle A. That's deep enough to feel "bass" but high enough to have definition. (Drop another octave to A0 and you'd lose audibility on small speakers; up at A2 it starts to sound like a synth lead instead of a bass.)

**Tip — Drum Rack and Bass live on different MIDI tracks.** Each instrument gets its own track. The MIDI note "A1" played on a bass synth → bass note. The same A1 played into a Drum Rack → whatever pad lives on A1 in that kit (often nothing, or open hat).

## This sets up sidechain ducking later

Even when kick and bass *do* overlap (in more elaborate basslines), producers use **sidechain compression** to make the bass briefly *duck* whenever the kick hits — automatically carving space. That's the famous "pumping" sound of dance music.

The kick/bass interlock pattern is the **rhythmic version** of that same idea. Learning the rhythmic version first makes the sidechain trick make sense later (Path 3 territory).

## Variations to try (after the basics feel solid)

- **Rolling 1/16 fill:** Add an extra bass 1/16 right before each kick (the "a" position) → fills the gap more aggressively.
- **Two-note bassline:** Replace one of the A's with a G or E for a tiny bit of melodic movement.
- **Skip a note:** Leave one of the four offbeat positions silent → breathing room, less relentless.

Keep these as variations on the foundation — don't ditch the offbeat pattern.
