# 🥁 Percussion Layering

Adding a **second, quieter percussion element** that fills the rhythmic gaps the main kit (kick / clap / hats) leaves open. It adds *movement* and *groove* without fighting the kick.

## The idea in one line

The kick/clap/hats are the **skeleton**; the percussion layer is the **connective tissue** that fills the micro-gaps so the loop feels like it's flowing instead of stamping.

## Why it doesn't crowd the kick

Two reasons it can sit *under* a busy four-on-the-floor without mud:

| Lever | What you do | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Frequency** | pick a high, short sound (shaker, rim, tambourine) | lives *above* the kick/bass, so it never competes for low-end space |
| **Volume** | keep velocity / fader low — texture, not a lead | the ear reads it as background shimmer, not a main hit |

This is the same logic as [[kick-bass-interlock|kick/bass interlock]] — instruments take turns in **time** *and* stay out of each other's way in **frequency**.

## Where the hits go

A typical kit covers the 8th-note grid: closed hat on the beats (`1 2 3 4`), open hat on the offbeats (`&`). The **16th-note in-betweens** — the `e` and `a` columns — are empty. That's exactly where a shaker running 16ths drops in:

```
              1 e & a 2 e & a 3 e & a 4 e & a
Kick        : ● · · · ● · · · ● · · · ● · · ·
Closed Hat  : ● · · · ● · · · ● · · · ● · · ·
Open Hat    : · · ● · · · ● · · · ● · · · ● ·
Shaker      : · ● · ● · ● · ● · ● · ● · ● · ●
```

The shaker takes every gap the hats leave → a continuous 16th-note shimmer that interlocks with everything else and gives the loop forward drive.

## Choosing the sound (genre-neutral → style notes)

| Sound | Feel | Common in |
|---|---|---|
| **Shaker** | continuous 16th shimmer | techno, house |
| **Rim / clave** | sparse, sharp accents (1–2 per bar) | latin house, afro, hip-hop |
| **Conga / bongo** | tonal, melodic-ish bounce | house, afro, disco |
| **Tambourine** | bright, jangly offbeats | disco, pop, gospel-house |

*Genre-neutral principle:* any short, high percussion sound placed in the gaps works. The **choice of sound** is what makes it style-specific.

## Two finishing moves

- **Velocity groove** — don't draw every shaker hit at the same loudness. Make the `a` hits a touch louder than the `e` hits (or vice-versa) and the line breathes instead of buzzing flatly.
- **Pan it off-center** — kick & bass stay dead-center; percussion can drift slightly left or right for width. Even ±15% opens up the stereo image.

## Per-scene variation

A clean move for a 4-scene arrangement:

| Scene | Shaker |
|---|---|
| Intro | off (let the kit breathe) |
| Build | quiet / sparse |
| Drop | full 16ths |
| Break | off or very sparse |

Bringing the percussion *in* at the Drop adds one more layer of energy exactly when you want it.
