# Master Limiter (Mix Safety Net)

A **Limiter** is a compressor turned up to an extreme: instead of gently squeezing loud parts, it puts a **hard ceiling** on the signal — nothing gets through above that level, no matter how hard it's pushed. It's the very last device before sound leaves Live.

## Why it matters here

Six tracks summing together on the Master can spike past 0 dB at moments even if each track individually looked fine — a kick, a stab hit, and a riser peak lining up on the same beat, for instance. The Limiter catches those spikes so they never clip, without you having to manually ride the Master fader.

## The recipe

1. Load **Limiter** (Audio Effects) as the **last device on the Master track**, after anything else there.
2. Set **Ceiling** to about **-0.3 dB** — the absolute maximum the output can ever reach, leaving a hair of headroom.
3. Leave **Gain** near 0 dB to start. Only raise it a small amount (1–3 dB) if the mix feels quiet — this pushes the average level up while the Ceiling still protects the peaks.

## What this isn't

This is a **safety net**, not full mastering. Cranking the Gain hard squashes all dynamics flat and starts sounding pumped/lifeless — a proper master (multiband compression, careful loudness targets) is a separate, later topic. For finishing this sketch, the Limiter's job is just: **stop it from clipping, add a touch of level.**

## Verify

Play the busiest section (Build 2) and watch the Master meter — it should sit comfortably without hitting the red, and the Limiter's own gain-reduction meter should only flicker on the loudest hits, not stay pinned down constantly.
