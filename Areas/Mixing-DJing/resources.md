# Resources & Tools

A running list of useful pages, discovery databases, and software for DJing and digging — what each one is and why it's worth using. Add new entries as dated/standalone sections below.

---

## Discogs — <https://www.discogs.com>

**What it is:** The biggest crowd-sourced music **database + marketplace** on the internet. Every release (vinyl, CD, cassette, digital) gets a catalog entry with full tracklists, credits, label, year, country, and details of each individual *pressing*.

**Why it's useful for DJing/digging:**
- **Discovery:** browse an artist's or label's complete discography in one place — great for going deeper than the few tracks you already know.
- **Track ID:** look up a release to get exact tracklists and version info when you're not sure what you're hearing.
- **Crate digging:** the **Marketplace** lets you buy/sell physical records; you can see what's rare, what it sells for, and find specific pressings.
- **Organization:** you can log what you own in a personal **Collection** and keep a **Wantlist** of stuff to hunt down.

**Beginner note:** a "pressing" just means a specific physical manufacturing run of a record — the same album can have many pressings (different years, countries, colors of vinyl), and Discogs tracks them all separately.

---

## Bandcamp — <https://bandcamp.com>

**What it is:** A storefront where **artists and labels sell music directly** — usually as high-quality downloads (you can grab WAV/FLAC, not just MP3) and physical/merch. Less of a database, more of a shop run by the musicians themselves.

**Why it's useful for DJing/digging:**
- **Discovery:** strong for **underground / independent** stuff that never reaches the big platforms. Tags and the "you might also like" links are good rabbit holes.
- **DJ-ready files:** you can buy **lossless** downloads (WAV/FLAC), which is what you want for mixing — better quality than streaming rips.
- **Supports artists:** the cut going to the musician is much higher than streaming, so it's the "ethical dig" option.
- **Bandcamp Daily / Bandcamp Friday:** editorial articles for discovery, and periodic days where the platform waives its fee.

**Beginner note:** *lossless* (WAV/FLAC) means the audio is stored with no quality thrown away, unlike MP3 which compresses by deleting detail. For DJing, lossless gives you cleaner sound, especially when you EQ or pitch tracks.

---

## Juno Download — <https://www.junodownload.com>

**What it is:** A dedicated **dance/electronic music download store** (sister site to the Juno Records vinyl shop). Catalog is organised the way DJs think — by genre, label, and release date.

**Why it's useful for DJing/digging:**
- **Genre-first browsing:** deep, specific genre categories (e.g. deep house, peak-time techno, drum & bass) with **"latest releases"** charts so you can keep up with what's current.
- **DJ charts & staff picks:** curated lists from DJs and the Juno team — a fast way to find tracks that work on a dancefloor.
- **Preview-friendly:** clear previews and key/BPM info on many releases, handy when building sets.
- **Lossless downloads:** WAV/FLAC available, like Bandcamp.

**Beginner note:** think of it as **Beatport's alternative** — a paid store aimed specifically at DJs buying individual tracks for sets, rather than a streaming subscription.
