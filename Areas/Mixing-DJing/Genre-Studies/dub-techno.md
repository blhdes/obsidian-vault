# Dub Techno

The **harmonic backbone** behind a huge chunk of deep electronic music — including [[ricardo-villalobos|Villalobos]]'s chords. If minimal is about *space and patience*, dub techno is where that idea was invented. One detuned chord, soaked in echo, pulsing forever.

**Typical BPM:** ~120–130 (often right around 125). Steady, mid-tempo, never rushed.
**Typical keys:** Minor / modal, but deliberately **blurry** — the signature chord is filtered and detuned so heavily that the exact key is ambiguous. Harmony is a *colour*, not a progression.
**Energy curve:** **Hypnotic and near-static.** A track can run 7–10 minutes and "change" only by a filter slowly opening or an echo feeding back. The reward is immersion, not release.
**Era / scene:** Born in **Berlin, early 1990s.** A fusion of **Detroit techno** + **Jamaican dub**. Still a living style today.

---

## Who started it

- **Basic Channel** — the duo **Moritz von Oswald** and **Mark Ernestus**, who founded the label of the same name in **Berlin around 1993.** They are *the* origin point. Their whole catalogue is essential.
- They built a small universe of aliases and labels:
  - **Maurizio** — a slightly more dancefloor-direct alias.
  - **Chain Reaction** (~1995) — the sister label that spread the sound: **Monolake** (Robert Henke — later a co-creator of **Ableton Live**, your DAW), **Porter Ricks**, Vainqueur, Fluxion, Substance.
  - **Rhythm & Sound** — their later project pulling the sound back toward **dub reggae** proper, with vocalists.
- The genius move: take Detroit techno's machine soul and run it through a **dub mixing desk** — endless tape echo, spring reverb, and live EQ muting. The *studio itself* became the instrument.

---

## The sound he... I mean *it* defined

**Dub** (the Jamaican production art, 1970s) = take a recording and remix it live: drop instruments in and out, drench everything in **echo and reverb**, ride the EQ and faders as a performance. The "empty space" and the decaying echoes *are* the music.

**Dub techno** = that approach applied to a 4/4 techno track. The result is deep, smoky, and architectural — like standing in a vast reverberant room while a single chord breathes.

---

## Sound signature / technical stuff

What makes a track read as *dub techno*:

- **THE chord stab.** A detuned, heavily **low-pass-filtered** chord drenched in **tape delay** — it pulses on the offbeat and slowly modulates. This single sound is the genre's calling card. Everything else is built around it.
- **Deep, round sub bass.** Felt more than heard. Simple, repeating, locked to the kick.
- **Soft, muffled 4/4 kick.** Often warm and rounded rather than punchy — it sits *in* the fog, not on top of it.
- **Tape hiss, crackle, vinyl noise.** Deliberate. "The sound of the room." Grit and warmth, never clinical.
- **Echo / delay feedback as performance.** Sounds are fed back into delays until they smear into atmosphere. Feedback is ridden live like an instrument.
- **Extreme repetition + micro-change.** Very few elements; the whole arc is a filter opening over minutes.

---

## A bit of theory

- **Space is an instrument.** Reverb tails and silence carry as much weight as the notes. Removing things is the main compositional move.
- **Static / ambiguous harmony.** Usually one minor/sus chord, so filtered you can't pin the exact notes. The *mood* (deep, melancholic, oceanic) is the point, not a chord change.
- **Performance > composition.** The "writing" happens live at the mixing desk — muting, EQ sweeps, delay feedback. It's dub philosophy: the mix is the song.
- **Repetition as meditation.** Tiny variation over long time creates trance. Your ear hunts for change and clings to every micro-shift.

---

## DJ angle

- **Tempo zone:** ~120–128. Sits perfectly under deep house, minimal, and [[ricardo-villalobos|Villalobos]]-style grooves.
- **The easiest music to blend.** Long beat-only intros/outros and those endless chord pads mean **two dub techno tracks melt into each other** almost effortlessly. A 2–4 minute blend feels natural, not lazy.
- **Use the chords to bridge, but mind the key.** Two heavily-reverbed chords *can* clash — if it sounds muddy or "wrong," EQ one chord down or pick the next track by feel.
- **Where it plays:** opening sets, deep late-night, comedowns, ambient-into-techno warm-ups. Sets a hypnotic, patient mood and buys you tons of room to be expressive.

**Transitions:**
- **From:** ambient / [[ambient]] drone → ease in a soft kick and the chord.
- **Into:** [[ricardo-villalobos|minimal / microhouse]] or deep house when you want to add groove and movement.
- **Into:** harder techno later by gradually swapping the soft kick for a punchier one.

## Listening list

Sorted roughly accessible → deeper.

### Tier 1 — the source
- **Basic Channel — *BCD* / *BCD-2*** (compilations) — the foundational tracks ("Phylyps Trak," "Q1.1," "Octagon"). Start here.
- **Maurizio — *Maurizio* (M-Series)** — the more dancefloor-facing side of the same brain.

### Tier 2 — the Chain Reaction universe
- **Monolake — *Hongkong*** (1997) — deep, detailed, the Robert Henke (Ableton) lineage.
- **Porter Ricks — *Biokinetics*** (1996) — oceanic, churning, more abstract dub techno.
- **Rhythm & Sound — *Rhythm & Sound*** (2001) — the dub-reggae-leaning evolution.

### Tier 3 — modern & adjacent
- **DeepChord presents Echospace — *The Coldest Season*** (2007) — the lush, modern American take (Rod Modell / Stephen Hitchell).
- **Pole — *1 / 2 / 3*** (1998–2000) — glitchy, crackly dub — the **bridge to microhouse** (Stefan Betke).
- **Yagya — *Rhythm of Snow*** — icy, melodic dub techno.
- **Gas — *Königsforst* / *Pop*** — dub-techno's ambient cousin (Wolfgang Voigt); more beatless but same hypnosis.
